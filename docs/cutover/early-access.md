# Early Access: the campaign switch, its time logic, its extension and how it ends (Production 20, 21)

Written for: the owner and the operator. Status: **built and tested locally; OFF by default; nothing is published** (Production 21 added the extension build mode, the guarantee and plan-change wording, the Plugin identity gate and a compact hero for Early Access builds). The Control Plane side (who may get access, the fixed expiry) is `docs/launch/early-access.md` in the Control Plane repository; this page is only what the static website says.

## 1. What it is

A fixed-calendar promotional access period, not a trial and not a permanent plan. Every Early Access store ends at the same instant, whatever its sign-up date: **2027-01-01 00:00:00 Asia/Jerusalem = 2026-12-31 22:00:00 UTC** (unix 1798754400). The last included moment is 31 December 2026, Israel time. Nothing renews or converts by itself; a paid plan starts only if the merchant chooses and buys it. This site decides only what the pages SAY; it cannot grant, extend or remove access. The **initial published end** above is never edited: a later end exists only as one of two published **extension steps** (section 7b), moved by an operator decision on the Control Plane first and followed by a Site rebuild.

## 2. The switch and its three states (build time)

`NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS`, separate from `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_COMMERCE_OPEN` (the campaign never opens paid checkout).

| Value | Mode | What a visitor sees |
|---|---|---|
| unset, or anything else | OFF | exactly the site as before: no trace of the campaign in any page |
| `announce` | ANNOUNCE | the offer, the end date, the planned price, the explanation, the questions; every button says Early Access opens soon (nothing to sign up for yet) |
| `open` | OPEN | the same, with buttons leading to the sign-up (`NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS_URL`; the Control Plane flow must exist and be approved) |

Only the exact words work: `true`, `1`, `OPEN`, ` open` are OFF.

## 3. Time logic (what keeps the page from going stale)

1. **Constants**, in `src/lib/earlyAccess.ts`: `EA_CUTOFF_UTC_MS` (the initial published end, 2026-12-31 22:00 UTC) and `EA_EXTENSION_STEPS` (step 1 = 2027-01-31 22:00 UTC = 2027-02-01 00:00 Israel, step 2 = 2027-02-28 22:00 UTC = 2027-03-01 00:00 Israel). `EA_END_UTC_MS` is the end of THIS build (its step's instant, or the initial cutoff) and is the only instant that means "the end" anywhere: build clock, head script, ended view, strike-through, every band, the build-clock refusal. `npm run ea:check` re-derives all three instants from the IANA time-zone database (not from the constants) under seven process time zones, compares them with the Control Plane constants (`CUTOFF_UNIX`, `EXTENSION_STEPS`) and fails if anything differs, so neither the machine nor a change of time rules can silently move them.
2. **Build guard.** A build made at or after ITS end (`EA_END_UTC_MS`) IGNORES the switch (mode OFF) and `npm run launch:check` reports the expired request. A build with no valid extension (step 0) made after the INITIAL cutoff is therefore OFF: the conservative side. A late deployment of an old configuration cannot publish an expired campaign.
3. **Page script.** On a campaign build, a 100-byte script in `<head>` compares `Date.now()` with the end of the build (absolute UTC milliseconds, no time zone, no locale). At or after the instant it marks `<html data-ea-over>`; the CSS then hides every offer block, shows the ordinary prices and "Purchases open soon" and a short "Early Access has ended" note. A page loaded from a CDN or browser cache after the end date therefore shows the ended view. A page left open across the instant keeps its view until it is reloaded.
4. **Limits, stated honestly.** A visitor without JavaScript, a crawler, a reader mode or an AI assistant that ignores the stylesheet sees the build as built (the offer AND the hidden ended note) until the site is redeployed, and search or assistant caches can keep the old text for days: **the redeploy in section 7 is mandatory, not optional**. A visitor with a wrong device clock sees the wrong view (display only: access itself ends on the servers). Meta descriptions, Open Graph text and structured data never mention the campaign, so nothing in a search result goes stale.
5. The campaign code never reads the visitor's time zone or formats a date with the locale (`ea:check` greps for it). Every statement of the END (bar, hero note, plan term and end lines, explanation, the end and plugin questions, the ended note) names its clock, and the statements of the last day are always next to one that does: "Israel time" and, on the plan cards and in the explanation, "22:00 UTC on December 31" (a merchant in Los Angeles loses access at 14:00 on December 31, and the copy says so). The build-clock pin for tests (`AICC_BUILD_NOW_MS`) works only together with `AICC_EA_TEST_SEAM=1`, and `launch:check` fails if either variable exists in a release environment.

## 4. Where it shows (all four languages; he is right-to-left)

Announcement bar under the header; the header button (OPEN: "Request Early Access", same destination as the hero button); Home hero buttons and fine print; the plan cards on Home and Pricing ("Free until December 31, 2026"; the plan price shown plainly as "Planned price when purchases open", or struck through with the label "Regular price, not charged during Early Access" only with `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EA_STRIKE=on`, which counsel must approve first and `launch:check` reports as a blocker; no card needed; the term and the end instant; one action per card); the Early Access explanation and its eight questions on Pricing (`#early-access`: free, end, automatic charge, how to continue, **can I change my plan**, **could it be extended**, the guarantee, what the plugin shows); the closing bands. The hero of an Early Access build is compacted while the offer is live so the whole headline, the primary action and the fine print are above the fold at 1280 x 720 and 390 x 844. Prices come only from `src/data/plans.ts` (Solo $99, Studio $199, Agency $499 a year, USD): the number shown is the real planned price, never a reference price, and no percentage, saving or discount wording exists. The Control Plane shows the same facts on its own pages and in its mail templates.

## 5. Enabling it (order matters; none of this is approved yet)

1. The Control Plane Early Access path is deployed dark and independently reviewed (Control Plane `docs/launch/early-access.md`).
2. The accountant has confirmed the proposed public activity in writing, counsel has approved the promotional terms, and the legal text on the site and in the Control Plane (Terms, Privacy, DPA, Refund) carries the Early Access disclosures.
3. Build with `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS=announce` first and publish it only on the owner's explicit approval; `launch:check` also requires `AICC_EA_PLUGIN_RELEASE_SHA256` (below); switch to `open` (with `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS_URL`) only when real merchants may be admitted.
4. `npm run launch:check`, `npm run ea:check -- --out out --expect <mode>`, `npm run test:early-access`.

**Plugin identity gate (Production 21).** In `announce` and `open` builds `launch:check` requires the environment variable `AICC_EA_PLUGIN_RELEASE_SHA256`: the sha256 (64 lower-case hex characters) of the Plugin ZIP that is distributed with Early Access. It must NOT equal the superseded candidate `0fee2c9d994cfe554103d78997be3a83a676dae8a9ee88bfd8319fcd9e9b5a83` (Plugin 1.1.0 candidate, which shows a 14-day money-back message on a free grant). It records that the corrected Plugin (`feat/p21-guarantee-fix`) is the one being handed to merchants; the owner supplies the value after the corrected ZIP is signed; nothing in the Site build reads it. The Early Access questions no longer mention the old message.

## 6. Approvals this needs from the owner (and the operating rules once it is live)

Publishing the `announce` build; publishing the `open` build; admitting real merchants (a Control Plane approval, not a site setting); the legal and accountant confirmations in section 5. `launch:check` reports the campaign as a blocker while it is on, so the existing production gate cannot pass by accident. **Operating rules while live:** (1) when the admission cap on the Control Plane is reached or requests stop being accepted, redeploy the same day as `announce` or without the switch: a static page cannot see the cap; (2) a named person (the owner) owns the post-cutoff redeploy of section 7 and puts it in their calendar for 2026-12-31 22:00 UTC (Jan 1, 00:00 in Israel); (3) the legal pages must carry the Early Access terms before `announce` is published (the page tells merchants these are the terms of the offer).

## 7. Ending it (the removal plan)

1. **Before 2027-01-01:** prepare the normal build (the default configuration, switch unset) and verify it with `ea:check -- --out <dir> --expect off`.
2. **At or just after 2027-01-01 00:00 Israel time (2026-12-31 22:00 UTC):** deploy the normal build. Until then the live campaign build stays; the page script already shows the ended view to anyone loading a page after the instant.
3. Verify on the live host: no "Early Access" text, no campaign prices, "Purchases open soon" (or the open checkout if Commerce has opened). The Mac sentinel's closed-commerce target keeps passing; add a post-cutoff check to your own calendar.
4. Remove `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS` and `..._URL` from the release environment file so no later build carries them (a build after the cutoff ignores them anyway).
5. Rollback of the campaign itself is the same deployment of the normal build; nothing in the Control Plane needs to change for the pages.

## 7b. Extending it (a contingency, never automatic)

An extension is a **contingency for the case that paid checkout is not ready**, decided by an authorised operator on the Control Plane (`wp aicc-ea extend --step=1|2 ...`, campaign-wide, no per-store extension, no operator-typed date, never a charge). The published terms already say so (the permanent reservation line in the terms and in the questions: "If we extend Early Access, we do it for every Early Access store at once and we say so on this page before the end date; where we can, we also tell you by e-mail. An extension never starts a charge. We cannot extend a single store's access."), so an extension is never a silent change.

**Order matters, and the Site is the LAST step.** A Site that announces a later date than the product delivers is the harmful order (merchants plan around a date the product does not honour); a product extended while the Site still shows the old date is the harmless order (the Site is merely stale for a while).
1. The Control Plane `extend` for the step is run and VERIFIED (its status shows the step ratified and fully applied; the Control Plane documentation is the authority). Nothing on this site can do that.
2. Only then rebuild the Site with BOTH `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EA_EXTENSION_STEP=1` (or `2`) and `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EA_EXTENSION_REF=<the operator's decision reference, 3-64 characters>` (letters, digits and `. _ : / # -`). Without the reference the step is ignored (the build keeps the initial end; `launch:check` says why). `launch:check` reports every extended build as a blocker until the operator confirms step 1 happened. Run `ea:check -- --out <dir> --expect <mode> --expect-step <n> --compare-off <normal build>` on the export (it proves the step's end in the head script, that none of the initial-end sentences is rendered, and that the head metadata is byte-identical with the normal build) and `test:early-access` with the four extended exports.
3. What an extended build says: the bar (`open`) "Early Access has been extended for every store: free until <last day>, Israel time."; one short note in the explanation ("first announced to end on December 31, 2026 ... nothing else changed: no payment is taken and nothing converts or is charged automatically"); every date in the cards, hero, explanation and questions moves to the step. An `announce` build keeps its own bar sentence with the new date plus the note.
4. The reservation promises the notice ON THIS PAGE before the end date (the Site rebuild of step 2 above, which must happen BEFORE the current end) and e-mail only "where we can": the Control Plane has no working e-mail sender today (its `extension_notice` builder exists, the transport is inert), so no e-mail may be assumed. If a notice mail can be sent, the operator sends it; if not, the page is the notice.
5. **Redeploy at the (extended) end is mandatory**, exactly as section 7, with the same rules: the owner (named) puts the end of the step actually in force in their calendar (2027-01-31 22:00 UTC for step 1, 2027-02-28 22:00 UTC for step 2), deploys the normal build at or just after that instant and verifies it; a build made at or after the end ignores the switch. Removing the extension variables is part of that deployment. A second step is decided the same way (step 1 first, then step 2), each before the current end.
6. Rolling back an extension of the Site means rebuilding without the variables, which would show the INITIAL end again: do that only if the Control Plane has also been returned to the initial end (the Control Plane documents that shortening path as an incident procedure); never leave the Site showing an earlier date than the product delivers without telling merchants.

The campaign vocabulary is allowed only under `src/i18n/ea/` (`scripts/lib/early-access-truth.mjs`).
