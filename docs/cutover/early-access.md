## 1. What it is

A fixed-calendar promotional access period, not a trial and not a permanent plan. Every the launch offer store ends at the same instant, whatever its sign-up date: **2027-01-01 00:00:00 Asia/Jerusalem = 2026-12-31 22:00:00 UTC** (unix 1798754400). The last included moment is 31 December 2026, Israel time. Nothing renews or converts by itself; a paid plan starts only if the merchant chooses and buys it. This site decides only what the pages SAY; it cannot grant, extend or remove access. The **initial published end** above is never edited: a later end exists only as one of two published **extension steps** (section 7b), moved by an operator decision on the Control Plane first and followed by a Site rebuild.

## 2. The switch and its three states (build time)

`NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS`, separate from `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_COMMERCE_OPEN` (the campaign never opens paid checkout).

| Value | Mode | What a visitor sees |
|---|---|---|
| unset, or anything else | OFF | exactly the site as before: no trace of the campaign in any page |
| `announce` | ANNOUNCE | see section 0 (P32: the offer, the end instant, the regular price, the explanation; every request control is a non-clickable status) |
| `open` | OPEN | see section 0 (P32: buttons lead to `/request-access/`; the attestations are required) |

Only the exact words work: `true`, `1`, `OPEN`, ` open` are OFF.

## 3. Time logic (what keeps the page from going stale)

1. **Constants**, in `src/lib/earlyAccess.ts`: `EA_CUTOFF_UTC_MS` (the initial published end, 2026-12-31 22:00 UTC) and `EA_EXTENSION_STEPS` (step 1 = 2027-01-31 22:00 UTC = 2027-02-01 00:00 Israel, step 2 = 2027-02-28 22:00 UTC = 2027-03-01 00:00 Israel). `EA_END_UTC_MS` is the end of THIS build (its step's instant, or the initial cutoff) and is the only instant that means "the end" anywhere: build clock, head script, ended view, strike-through, every band, the build-clock refusal. `npm run ea:check` re-derives all three instants from the IANA time-zone database (not from the constants) under seven process time zones, compares them with the Control Plane constants (`CUTOFF_UNIX`, `EXTENSION_STEPS`) and fails if anything differs, so neither the machine nor a change of time rules can silently move them.
2. **Build guard.** A build made at or after ITS end (`EA_END_UTC_MS`) IGNORES the switch (mode OFF) and `npm run launch:check` reports the expired request. A build with no valid extension (step 0) made after the INITIAL cutoff is therefore OFF: the conservative side. A late deployment of an old configuration cannot publish an expired campaign.
3. **Page script.** On a campaign build, a 100-byte script in `<head>` compares `Date.now()` with the end of the build (absolute UTC milliseconds, no time zone, no locale). At or after the instant it marks `<html data-ea-over>`; the CSS then hides every offer block, shows the ordinary prices and "Purchases open soon" and a short "The launch offer has ended" note. A page loaded from a CDN or browser cache after the end date therefore shows the ended view. A page left open across the instant keeps its view until it is reloaded.
4. **Limits, stated honestly.** A visitor without JavaScript, a crawler, a reader mode or an AI assistant that ignores the stylesheet sees the build as built (the offer AND the hidden ended note) until the site is redeployed, and search or assistant caches can keep the old text for days: **the redeploy in section 7 is mandatory, not optional**. A visitor with a wrong device clock sees the wrong view (display only: access itself ends on the servers). Meta descriptions, Open Graph text and structured data never mention the campaign, so nothing in a search result goes stale.
5. The campaign code never reads the visitor's time zone or formats a date with the locale (`ea:check` greps for it). Every statement of the END (bar, hero note, plan term and end lines, explanation, the end and plugin questions, the ended note) names its clock, and the statements of the last day are always next to one that does: "Israel time" and, on the plan cards and in the explanation, "22:00 UTC on December 31" (a merchant in Los Angeles loses access at 14:00 on December 31, and the copy says so). The build-clock pin for tests (`AICC_BUILD_NOW_MS`) works only together with `AICC_EA_TEST_SEAM=1`, and `launch:check` fails if either variable exists in a release environment.

## 4. Where it shows (all four languages; he is right-to-left)

Announcement bar under the header; the header button (OPEN: "Request the launch offer", same destination as the hero button); Home hero buttons and fine print; the plan cards on Home and Pricing ("Free until December 31, 2026"; the plan price shown plainly as "Planned price when purchases open", or struck through with the label "Regular price, not charged during the launch offer" only with `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EA_STRIKE=on`, which counsel must approve first and `launch:check` reports as a blocker; no card needed; the term and the end instant; one action per card); the the launch offer explanation and its eight questions on Pricing (`#early-access`: free, end, automatic charge, how to continue, **can I change my plan**, **could it be extended**, the guarantee, what the plugin shows); the closing bands. The hero of an campaign build is compacted while the offer is live so the whole headline, the primary action and the fine print are above the fold at 1280 x 720 and 390 x 844. Prices come only from `src/data/plans.ts` (Solo $99, Studio $199, Agency $499 a year, USD): the number shown is the real planned price, never a reference price, and no percentage, saving or discount wording exists. The Control Plane shows the same facts on its own pages and in its mail templates.

## 5. Enabling it (order matters; none of this is approved yet)

1. The Control Plane the launch offer path is deployed dark and independently reviewed (Control Plane `docs/launch/early-access.md`).
2. The accountant has confirmed the proposed public activity in writing, counsel has approved the promotional terms, and the legal text on the site and in the Control Plane (Terms, Privacy, DPA, Refund) carries the the launch offer disclosures.
3. Build with `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS=announce` first and publish it only on the owner's explicit approval; `launch:check` also requires `AICC_EA_PLUGIN_RELEASE_SHA256` (below); switch to `open` (with `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS_URL`) only when real merchants may be admitted.
4. `npm run launch:check`, `npm run ea:check -- --out out --expect <mode>`, `npm run test:early-access`.

**Plugin identity gate (Production 21).** In `announce` and `open` builds `launch:check` requires the environment variable `AICC_EA_PLUGIN_RELEASE_SHA256`: the sha256 (64 lower-case hex characters) of the Plugin ZIP that is distributed with the launch offer. It must NOT equal the superseded candidate `0fee2c9d994cfe554103d78997be3a83a676dae8a9ee88bfd8319fcd9e9b5a83` (Plugin 1.1.0 candidate, which shows a 14-day money-back message on a free grant). It records that the corrected Plugin (`feat/p21-guarantee-fix`) is the one being handed to merchants; the owner supplies the value after the corrected ZIP is signed; nothing in the Site build reads it. The the launch offer questions no longer mention the old message.

## 6. Approvals this needs from the owner (and the operating rules once it is live)

Publishing the `announce` build; publishing the `open` build; admitting real merchants (a Control Plane approval, not a site setting); the legal and accountant confirmations in section 5. `launch:check` reports the campaign as a blocker while it is on, so the existing production gate cannot pass by accident. **Operating rules while live:** (1) when the admission cap on the Control Plane is reached or requests stop being accepted, redeploy the same day as `announce` or without the switch: a static page cannot see the cap; (2) a named person (the owner) owns the post-cutoff redeploy of section 7 and puts it in their calendar for 2026-12-31 22:00 UTC (Jan 1, 00:00 in Israel); (3) the legal pages must carry the the launch offer terms before `announce` is published (the page tells merchants these are the terms of the offer).

## 7. Ending it (the removal plan)

1. **Before 2027-01-01:** prepare the normal build (the default configuration, switch unset) and verify it with `ea:check -- --out <dir> --expect off`.
2. **At or just after 2027-01-01 00:00 Israel time (2026-12-31 22:00 UTC):** deploy the normal build. Until then the live campaign build stays; the page script already shows the ended view to anyone loading a page after the instant.
3. Verify on the live host: no "launch offer" text, no campaign prices, "Purchases open soon" (or the open checkout if Commerce has opened). The Mac sentinel's closed-commerce target keeps passing; add a post-cutoff check to your own calendar.
4. Remove `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS` and `..._URL` from the release environment file so no later build carries them (a build after the cutoff ignores them anyway).
5. Rollback of the campaign itself is the same deployment of the normal build; nothing in the Control Plane needs to change for the pages.

## 7b. Extending it (a contingency, never automatic)

An extension is a **contingency for the case that paid checkout is not ready**, decided by an authorised operator on the Control Plane (`wp aicc-ea extend --step=1|2 ...`, campaign-wide, no per-store extension, no operator-typed date, never a charge). The published terms already say so (the permanent reservation line in the terms and in the questions: "If we extend the launch offer, we do it for every the launch offer store at once and we say so on this page before the end date; where we can, we also tell you by e-mail. An extension never starts a charge. We cannot extend a single store's access."), so an extension is never a silent change.

**The Control Plane takes no new request after the INITIAL end.** Its grant window is pinned to the initial cutoff (2027-01-01 00:00 Israel), whatever extension is in effect, and so is its 24-hour minimum. An extended OPEN build therefore behaves in two phases, switched by the same fixed-instant head script (no fetch): **before the initial end** the page offers requests exactly as before (the explanation says the extension moved the end for everyone, and that nothing is granted in the final 24 hours before the original end date); **from the initial end until the extended end** every request control is replaced by "New requests are closed" wording (header button becomes "How it works", hero primary action "How the launch offer works", plan cards "New requests are closed", the "Request access" step "New requests are closed", the offer lines say the extension is for stores already in the launch offer) and the bar and a second note say plainly that new requests closed at the original end date. After the extended end the ordinary ended view returns. An `announce` build has no request control and admits nobody, so it has no closed phase: its bar and note say the end date MOVED ("The launch offer is coming and its end date has moved ... Nothing can be requested yet"), never "extended for every store" (the cards keep saying the launch offer opens soon). In the closed phase the Pricing explanation also drops the eligibility line, "We review each request" and "Ask for the plan you would want to keep" (replaced by "New requests are closed" and the plan-change line for stores already in), and the bar link disappears (the header button and the hero action lead to the same place). In the last 24 hours before the initial end the Control Plane already refuses grants while the page (every OPEN build, as in Production 20) still shows the button: the offer line now says "Requests close 24 hours before it ends" ("...before the original end date" in an extended build) and the questions repeat the rule.

**Without JavaScript** (or with the inline head script blocked) the phase cannot be applied: the static HTML is the live, requests-open view, so the request links of an extended OPEN build stay visible after the initial end (5 on Pricing) although the Control Plane refuses them. This is the same documented limit as the ended view (section 3, item 4), not a defect to fix on a static site; the mandatory redeploy at the end and the cache rules below are what bound it. The Product page hero keeps the ordinary "Get started" button in every phase (pre-existing, unconditional): it leads to the account area (`/my-account/`, plain, no `offer=early-access`), where public registration is off while Commerce is closed; it is not a request control and is not changed.

**Order matters, and the Site is the LAST step.** A Site that announces a later date than the product delivers is the harmful order (merchants plan around a date the product does not honour); a product extended while the Site still shows the old date is the harmless order (the Site is merely stale for a while). Procedure:
1. The Control Plane `extend` for the extension step is run and VERIFIED (its status shows the step ratified and fully applied; the Control Plane documentation is the authority). Nothing on this site can do that.
2. Rebuild the Site with BOTH `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EA_EXTENSION_STEP=1` (or `2`) and `NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EA_EXTENSION_REF=<the operator's decision reference, 3-64 characters>` (letters, digits and `. _ : / # -`; obvious placeholders such as todo, tbd, xxx, test, placeholder, n/a or one repeated character are refused). Without a valid reference the step is ignored (the build keeps the initial end; `launch:check` says why). Run `i18n:strict` ONCE MORE with the release environment (`NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EARLY_ACCESS=open NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EA_EXTENSION_STEP=<n> NEXT_PUBLIC_AICC_EA_EXTENSION_REF=<ref> npm run i18n:strict`): it compares the rendered the launch offer text with the authored text of THAT step, which the default environment cannot see. The reference is never rendered: `ea:check -- --out <dir> ... --ref <the reference>` proves it appears in no exported file. Run `ea:check -- --out <dir> --expect <mode> --expect-step <n> --compare-off <normal build>` on the export (it proves the step's end and the initial end in the head script, that none of the initial-end sentences is rendered, and that the head metadata is byte-identical with the normal build) and `test:early-access` with the four extended exports.
3. **The HTML must not be served stale (required).** The head script compares the visitor's clock with the end BAKED INTO THE PAGE: a copy of the INITIAL (step 0) page that is still cached in a browser or on a CDN flips to "The launch offer has ended" at 2026-12-31 22:00 UTC although access is extended, and the page promises "we say so on this page". So, for the whole extension window: serve every HTML page with a short or no-cache `Cache-Control` (`no-cache`, `no-store`, or `max-age` of at most 300 s; no `s-maxage` above 300; no `Cache-Control` at all is NOT acceptable: a browser then applies heuristic freshness to an old Last-Modified); **purge the HTML cache and the CDN at the extension deploy**; and run the post-deploy check against the live origin from the owner's machine: `npm run ea:live -- https://www.copilt.org --step <n>` (read-only GETs; it fails on a long-lived or missing `Cache-Control` and on a served page that does not carry the head script of that step; the same by hand: `curl -sI https://www.copilt.org/ https://www.copilt.org/pricing/ | grep -i cache-control`, then check the served `Date.now()>=<end of the step>` in the page source). **Residual risk, stated plainly:** a visitor whose browser or an intermediate proxy kept a step-0 page that was fetched before the deploy and is still fresh by its own rules will see "ended" at the initial end until it revalidates; the short TTL and the purge limit that to the TTL after the deploy, they cannot reach a copy already in a browser with a long freshness lifetime that was cached before the headers were fixed (hence: set the short-TTL header from the FIRST campaign deployment, not only at the extension).
4. `launch:check` reports two explicit blockers for every extended build, each cleared only by the exact word `yes` in its own variable: `AICC_EA_EXTENSION_CP_VERIFIED=yes` (the Control Plane `extend` of THIS step has been run and verified, item 1) and `AICC_EA_HTML_NOCACHE_CONFIRMED=yes` (item 3 done and `ea:live` passed). The generic blockers of any campaign build (owner approval, the request intake) are separate and stay.
5. What an extended build says: the bar (`open`) "The launch offer has been extended for every store: free until <last day>, Israel time." (and, from the initial end, "...for every store already in it ... New requests closed at the original end date."); in `announce` builds the same extended bar; the extension note ("first announced to end on December 31, 2026, Israel time ... nothing else changed: no payment is taken and nothing converts or is charged automatically") on Home and in the Pricing explanation; every date in the cards, hero, explanation and questions moves to the step.
6. The reservation promises the notice ON THIS PAGE before the end date (the Site rebuild of item 2, which must happen BEFORE the current end) and e-mail only "where we can": the Control Plane has no working e-mail sender today (its `extension_notice` builder exists, the transport is inert), so no e-mail may be assumed. If a notice mail can be sent, the operator sends it; if not, the page is the notice.
7. **Redeploy at the (extended) end is mandatory**, exactly as section 7, with the same rules: the owner (named) puts the end of the step actually in force in their calendar (2027-01-31 22:00 UTC for step 1, 2027-02-28 22:00 UTC for step 2), deploys the normal build at or just after that instant and verifies it; a build made at or after the end ignores the switch. Removing the extension variables is part of that deployment. A second step is decided the same way (step 1 first, then step 2), each before the current end.
8. Rolling back an extension of the Site means rebuilding without the variables, which would show the INITIAL end again: do that only if the Control Plane has also been returned to the initial end (the Control Plane documents that shortening path as an incident procedure); never leave the Site showing an earlier date than the product delivers without telling merchants.

**Plugin release identity (`AICC_EA_PLUGIN_RELEASE_SHA256`) and its allow-list.** `launch:check` accepts only a hash in `scripts/data/ea-plugin-releases.json` (and never one of its `superseded` entries, never 64 zeros). The first entry is the current UNSIGNED candidate (`3f7a85ea...c1b6`); while the only allow-listed hash is unsigned, `launch:check` reports it as an UNSIGNED candidate blocker. **Adding or replacing an entry is a conscious act**, done in its own commit, only when a new Plugin build has been attested (built from the corrected source, ZIP hashed, and for a release signed). The value is recorded, not verified against any file, and the Site build never reads it.

**The Control Plane constants cross-check.** `ea:check` compares `CUTOFF_UNIX` and `EXTENSION_STEPS` with the Control Plane worktree `~/Local Sites/woo-recommendation-qa/aicc-control-plane-worktrees/p21-early-access` (branch `feat/p21-early-access`, head 0579a31 when this was written; override with `AICC_CP_DIR`). It FAILS when the file is missing; `AICC_CP_DIR=none` disables it explicitly (a machine without a Control Plane checkout; never in a release run). The comparison is proven to fail on wrong constants by negative controls on every run.
